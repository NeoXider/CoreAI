# Block World

A small Minecraft-style game whose logic and interface are entirely Lua mods on CoreAI's Roblox-shaped API:
a seeded voxel world in `workspace.Terrain` with caves, building and breaking at the crosshair, a hotbar, day and
night, torches, survival with health and falls, villagers who talk, and an autopilot. It is also the template for "a game like this": every part is a
short mod you can read in one sitting, change while the game runs, or ask the AI to change.

## Open and play

Open `Assets/CoreAI.Demos/BlockWorld/BlockWorld.unity` and press Play. It plays with no AI model configured;
the features that need one say so (see [What needs a model](#what-needs-a-model)).

- **Without Play** the scene shows the spawn area: a preview the scene builder baked by running the world mod
  once. It is tagged EditorOnly, removes itself when Play starts, and never ships in a player.
- **At Play** the world is generated by the mods from the same seed: the column you stand on first (the
  character waits there, held, for the third of a second it takes), then the rest around you. A fresh world
  starts at 10:00, a mid-morning sun that lights the hills and still gives them shadows, and you stand in third
  person behind your character, facing the most telling view from the spawn (a hill, the sea, its sandy shore).
- **Rebuild the scene** with `CoreAI/Demos/Build Block World Demo`, or in batch mode with
  `-executeMethod CoreAI.Demos.BlockWorld.BlockWorldDemoSceneBuilder.BuildScene`. The scene and its preview are a
  function of the builder (`Editor/BlockWorldDemoSceneBuilder.cs`) and of the world mod.

What the scene holds: one `CoreAI Play World` object, the one-click play world (`Docs/CoreAIMods/PLAY_WORLD.md`)
with the controls and default camera, the default look and the in-world chat, plus `BlockWorldDemoModule`; one
`Sun` (it is `RenderSettings.sun`, the light the clock turns); the camera at the spawn. There is no baseplate:
the Terrain is the ground. The scene keeps its own mods store (`block-world-demo`), so its saved world never
opens in another demo.

`BlockWorldDemoModule` (`Scripts/`) is the only scene glue and holds no game rule. It turns the framework setting
`CoreAiLuaAiOptions.AllowAgentsFromLua` on for this scene's mods scope (it stays off in every other scene), and on
the first frame of the published world it switches the `Samples/Block World` folder on in this scene's store and
loads what the start-up rehydrate did not. It does that at every start, so switching one of the eight mods off in
the Hub lasts until the next start.

In a WebGL player the module sets `BlockWorldViewRadius` to 3 columns before the world mod loads.

## Controls

The key legend is on screen at the bottom right.

| Input | What it does |
|---|---|
| W A S D / arrows | Walk (the bundled `coreai_player_controls` mod) |
| Space | Jump; double-tap to fly, then Space rises and Left Shift sinks |
| Mouse | Look (the pointer is locked) |
| Left click | Break the outlined block (hold to keep breaking); on a torch, take it back |
| Right click | Place the hotbar block on the outlined face (hold to keep placing) |
| 1 - 9, mouse wheel | Choose a hotbar slot (or click a slot while the pointer is free); slot 9 is the torch |
| G | Creative (endless blocks, no damage) or survival (you place what you broke, and falls hurt) |
| V | Third person (the start) or first person |
| E | Talk to the villager you look at, or the nearest one within 14 studs |
| P, or `/autopilot on`, `/autopilot off`, `/autopilot status` | Autopilot on or off; any movement key also takes the controls back |
| L | Time of day: noon, dusk, midnight, dawn |
| T or Enter | Open the chat; `/` opens it with a command started |
| `/fly`, `/undo`, `/redo` | Chat commands of the play world (flight, and the AI's last change) |

Reach is five blocks, measured from your character's eyes. Bedrock, the bottom layer, cannot be broken, and a
block is never placed where you stand.

## Prompts to try

Point the crosshair at the ground first: the AI builds where you look, in cells.

- "Build a watchtower here." Then `/undo` takes it back, and `/redo` puts it back.
- "Put a small brick house with a door on this spot."
- "Make a staircase from here up to the top of that hill."
- "Dig a 5 x 5 pool here and fill it with water."
- "Light this cave: put torches every few blocks along the walls."
- "Make it night." or "Make the days twice as long."
- "Give the hotbar a darker look with gold borders." (the HUD is UXML and USS in `blockworld_hud`)
- "Tobin, what should I build next?" (said in the chat: a line that names a villager within 40 studs of you goes
  to that villager instead of the AI; so does a follow-up to the one you just talked to, or a line said near one)

## Survival

Press G to leave creative. In survival:

- Breaking a block puts it in your hotbar count and placing one takes it; the counts show on the hotbar slots.
- Your health (the Humanoid's own `Health` of 100) shows at the top left. A fall of up to three blocks is free;
  each stud beyond costs 2.5 health, so about thirteen blocks kill. Flying never counts as falling.
- At zero health you die: a notice says so and after three seconds you are back at the spawn with full health.
- Press G again and all of it stops: no damage, endless blocks, health back to full.

Not built: mobs, hunger and crafting. The game has none of them today and does not pretend to.

## Caves

Under the hills run winding tunnels with a few larger chambers, five to twelve blocks down; now and then a
tunnel reaches the surface, so you find a way in by walking around. A cave at the shore or under the sea is
flooded up to the sea level, inland caves are dry, no cave cuts through the bedrock, and the ground around the
spawn stays whole. Under rock the clock gives the night's ambient light, so a torch is what lights a cave.

## When /undo works

`/undo` takes back your latest AI change as a whole, and `/redo` puts it back, as long as nothing else in the
saved world changed since. Walking, flying, looking around, the clock, the villagers and the autopilot's walking
do not count. Breaking or placing a block, putting down or taking back a torch, or another AI change after it
does: then `/undo` changes nothing and says why, and the World Loads page in the Hub restores an autosave
instead. So try it right after the AI built: "Build a watchtower here", look at it, `/undo`, `/redo`.

## What needs a model

| Feature | Without a model | With a model |
|---|---|---|
| Walking, flying, building, breaking, hotbar, day and night, torches | works | works |
| "Build here" and other chat requests | the chat answers that no AI model is set up and where to choose one | the AI writes cells at the crosshair; `/undo` restores them |
| Villagers | stand by the spawn and answer that no model answered, in the chat and a bubble | answer by name, in the chat and a bubble, and remember you |
| Autopilot | a fixed rule keeps the current goal, else wanders | the decision model picks the goal from the mod's list |

Villagers also need the game to allow AI from Lua, the framework setting `CoreAiLuaAiOptions.AllowAgentsFromLua`.
It is off in a game unless the game turns it on, and this demo's scene turns it on. With it off the villagers
stand still and say why, naming the setting. The autopilot walks for wander, follow and idle itself; gather (break
nearby wood and stone), build (a small plank shelter with a doorway) and home are carried out by the mod.

## How the mods are organised

Eight bundled mods in one mod folder, `Samples/Block World` (their `category:`), under
`Assets/CoreAIMods/Runtime/Resources/CoreAIMods/BlockWorld/`. Turn the folder on as a whole; each mod also
reloads on its own.

| Mod | Owns | Talks to the others through |
|---|---|---|
| `blockworld_world` | the seeded world: hills, sea, shore, stone, caves, bedrock, trees, streamed around you in budgeted steps (your own column first, then the one whose edge is nearest you, what is in front of the camera before what is behind); the spawn and the heading you first face | `BlockWorldSeed`, `BlockWorldSpawn`, `BlockWorldSpawnLook`, `BlockWorldReady`, `BlockWorldColumns` |
| `blockworld_build` | the crosshair target and its outline, breaking and placing, reach, creative or survival, the view | `CrosshairCell`, `CrosshairPlaceCell`, `CrosshairNormal`, `CrosshairPartName`, `CrosshairModelName`, `BlockWorldCreative`, `BlockWorldPlaced`, `BlockWorldBroken`, `BlockWorldCount_<Material>` |
| `blockworld_hud` | the whole interface (UXML and USS text through `UxmlService`), the key legend, health in survival and the hotbar choice | `BlockWorldSelected`, `BlockWorldSlot` |
| `blockworld_daynight` | the clock (`Lighting.ClockTime`, a fresh world starts at 10:00), the night ambient and the dark under rock, `/time` (a `TextChatCommand`) and the L key | `BlockWorldTime` (set it to "noon", "18:30", "stop", "go"), `BlockWorldUnderground` |
| `blockworld_torches` | torches with a PointLight and a flame, the light budget | `BlockWorldTorches` |
| `blockworld_villagers` | Mira, Tobin and Elda: character Models under `workspace` with Roblox's R6 body (`Head`, `Torso`, `Left Arm` ...), which the platform's character rig animates as it does the player's, held until the ground under them exists, their `AIService` agents and brains | on each Model `NpcAgent`, `NpcBrain`, `NpcState` (the host writes `NpcAction`); `BlockWorldVillagerReply` |
| `blockworld_survival` | health from the Humanoid, fall damage, death and the respawn at the spawn; off in creative | `BlockWorldHealth`, `BlockWorldMaxHealth`, `BlockWorldDeaths` |
| `blockworld_autopilot` | the block world's goals and facts for the game's autopilot, and the goals only a block world knows: gather, build, home | on the Player `Autopilot`, `AutopilotGoals`, `AutopilotState` (the host writes `AutopilotGoal`; the mod writes the Player only while the autopilot drives, so `/undo` keeps working); `BlockWorldAutopilot` |

Why this cut: each mod is one thing a player can see and one thing you might want to replace. The mods never
call each other (Lua mods have no `require` here, and they do not use `BindableEvent`, which mods can use
between them now); they share state the Roblox way, as attributes on `workspace`, written by exactly one owner and read by anyone. Any notice a mod
wants shown goes to `BlockWorldNotice`, which the HUD displays. So you can drop the HUD and write your own,
replace the generator with a flat world, or remove the villagers, and the rest keeps working.

Knobs you can set as workspace attributes before the world mod loads: `BlockWorldSeed` (another world),
`BlockWorldViewRadius` (1 to 8 columns of 64 studs; smaller for WebGL).

Not done on purpose today: the mods still share state through workspace attributes. `BindableEvent` and
`BindableFunction` exist now, and moving the channels onto them is a possible cleanup, not a change this demo needs.

## Budgets

- World generation spends a fixed amount of work per frame, counted in cells before a pillar is filled (a pillar
  costs its cells, eight more, and up to 24 for its caves; 250 a frame). The fullest frame stays near 4,000
  instructions, under half the per-resume budget every Heartbeat handler gets (10,000). Nothing is generated
  while the mod loads (a reload must fit an AI tool call); the spawn column, which has no caves, comes first, in
  under forty frames, and a column with caves takes about fifty.
- The torch mod keeps at most 48 lit torches and says so when it is full. The host adds its own light budget:
  desktop (Forward+) keeps 64 lights, the WebGL tier keeps the 8 nearest on and reports it.
- The HUD rewrites a label only when its text changes, so an idle frame writes nothing to the UI.
